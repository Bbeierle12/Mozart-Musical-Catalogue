Biography

Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart (1756-1791) was a prolific and profoundly influential composer of the Classical period. Born in Salzburg, he displayed prodigious musical gifts from earliest childhood—competent on keyboard and violin and composing by the age of five. As a boy he toured the courts of Europe with his father Leopold, astonishing audiences with his abilities.

Over a career of barely three decades, Mozart composed more than 600 works spanning virtually every genre of his era: symphonies, concertos, operas, chamber music, sacred works, and solo keyboard music. He absorbed and synthesised the styles of his contemporaries while learning counterpoint from his study of J. S. Bach and Handel, fusing galant grace with contrapuntal depth.

From 1781 he lived in Vienna as a freelance musician, where he produced many of his best-known works—the operas Le nozze di Figaro, Don Giovanni, and Die Zauberflöte, the great piano concertos, and the final symphonic trilogy of 1788. He died in 1791 at the age of 35, leaving his Requiem unfinished. He is universally regarded as one of the greatest composers in the history of Western music.

Symphonies (41 Numbered Works)

Mozart composed roughly 41 numbered symphonies, from childhood essays in the form to the towering achievements of his final years. The culmination came in the summer of 1788, when within a few weeks he produced the final trilogy—Nos. 39, 40, and 41—without any known commission.

The Final Trilogy (1788)

In an astonishing burst of creativity, Mozart completed three symphonies in roughly six weeks: No. 39 in E-flat (warm and lyrical), No. 40 in G minor (urgent and tragic), and No. 41 in C major, the 'Jupiter', whose finale weaves five themes into a triumphant fugal climax.

Operas & Stage Works

Mozart's operas span the Italian opera seria and opera buffa traditions and the German Singspiel. Several rank among the most performed and admired works in the entire operatic repertory, distinguished by their psychological insight and the seamless union of music and drama.

The Da Ponte Collaborations

Three operas to libretti by Lorenzo Da Ponte stand among the summits of the form, combining comedy, tenderness, and dramatic depth.

Die Zauberflöte, K. 620

Singspiel • 1791

A fairy-tale opera blending Masonic symbolism, comedy, and sublime music, from the Queen of the Night's stratospheric coloratura to Sarastro's solemn bass arias. It was premiered just weeks before Mozart's death.

Concertos

Mozart brought the Classical concerto—above all the piano concerto—to its highest form. He wrote 27 piano concertos, 5 violin concertos, and concertos for clarinet, horn, flute, oboe, and bassoon, alongside the Sinfonia Concertante for violin and viola.

Piano Concertos

The mature Viennese piano concertos balance symphonic richness with operatic lyricism, the soloist conversing with a fully independent orchestra.

Clarinet Concerto in A major, K. 622

1791 • Three movements

Written for the clarinettist Anton Stadler and among the last works Mozart completed. Its serene Adagio is one of the most beloved slow movements in all music.

Chamber Music

Mozart's chamber music includes 23 string quartets, the great string quintets, and works pairing winds with strings. The six quartets dedicated to Haydn marked a new standard of contrapuntal mastery in the genre.

Sacred & Choral Music

Mozart composed masses, motets, and other sacred works throughout his life, from his Salzburg church duties to the unfinished masterpiece of his final months.

The Requiem, K. 626

Mozart's Final Work

Commissioned anonymously in 1791 (by Count Franz von Walsegg, who intended to pass it off as his own), the Requiem occupied Mozart in his last weeks. He left it unfinished at his death; his pupil Franz Xaver Süssmayr completed it from Mozart's sketches and instructions.

Key: D minor

Scoring: SATB soloists and chorus, basset horns, bassoons, trumpets, trombones, timpani, strings, organ

1783 • Three movements
