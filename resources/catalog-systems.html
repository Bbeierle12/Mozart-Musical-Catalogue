Because composers rarely assigned opus numbers consistently, musicologists created thematic catalogues to identify and order each composer's works. These numbering systems are now the standard way to refer to a piece unambiguously.

How They Work

Köchel (K.) — Mozart

The Köchel catalogue numbers Mozart's works roughly in chronological order, from K. 1 (childhood pieces) to K. 626 (the Requiem). Later revisions added or reordered entries, so some works carry compound numbers (e.g. K. 297b).

BWV — Bach

Unlike Köchel, the Bach-Werke-Verzeichnis groups works by genre rather than chronology: cantatas first (BWV 1–224), then motets, masses, organ works, keyboard works, and so on. A BWV number therefore tells you the kind of piece as much as its identity.

HWV — Handel

The Händel-Werke-Verzeichnis also organises by category: operas (HWV 1–42), oratorios, odes, sacred music, instrumental works, and more.

RV — Vivaldi

Peter Ryom's catalogue replaced earlier inconsistent systems. It is arranged by instrumentation and genre—concertos, sinfonias, sonatas, sacred vocal works, and operas—rather than by date.

Hoboken (Hob.) — Haydn

Hoboken's catalogue uses Roman-numeral group categories (e.g. Hob. I for symphonies, Hob. III for string quartets) followed by a number within the group.
